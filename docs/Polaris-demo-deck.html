02
The problem

Every event calendar is a
snapshot of a week that already ended.

Austin's dance calendar is genuinely good — 70 upcoming socials across 46 venues, maintained by hand. But venues don't maintain calendars. They post to Instagram.

Monday

The calendar says the two-step night runs 7pm, $10, every week.

Thursday

The venue posts its weekly rundown: "7/13, Monday: CLOSED."

Friday

You drive across Austin, park, and find a dark room. Nobody told the calendar.

The data isn't missing. It's just in the wrong place, in prose, on 26 different accounts.

03
What Polaris does

Turns Instagram captions into a verdict on every event.

Every event carries a live status, and every status links to the post that produced it. No trust required — tap the badge, read the caption yourself.

04
Architecture

Apify is the agent's eyes.
Elastic is its memory.

Seed Public event calendars The canonical listings, parsed straight from the page — including the Instagram handle behind each venue.
Apify · eyes Instagram scraper 26 venue accounts, newest posts on demand. Real-time, pay-per-run, no scraper infrastructure.
Elastic · memory events + ig_posts Every post and every verdict is a document. Kill any process, restart — nothing is lost.
Agent · judgment Reconciler Splits weekly-rundown captions per night, then rules on each event and writes back the evidence.
Surface Polaris app Tonight · Ask · Saved. Plus a dark city map of what's actually on.

The reconciler is deliberately rule-based, not an LLM: every verdict quotes the exact substring that caused it, so a human can audit any call in one glance.

05
Why Elastic

Search index and agent state, in one store.

Hybrid retrieval

Meaning, not just keywords

One semantic_text field gives ELSER embeddings at ingest — zero embedding code. /ask runs RRF over BM25 + semantic.

"beginner friendly country night" → Little Longhorn, which shares not one keyword with the query.

Agent memory

State that survives the process

Perception (ig_posts) and judgment (status, evidence, last_checked) are documents, not variables. Document ids are post URLs, so re-runs are idempotent.

Geo

The map comes free

geo_point per venue drives the city map today, and "what's on near me" next.

Kibana

Watching the agent think

Verdict breakdown and posts-per-handle, live, with no dashboard code written.

06
Why Apify

Instagram, without owning a scraper.

$0.008 per profile run, measured
26 accounts watched
28s scrape to indexed
  • Instagram actively fights scrapers. Apify absorbs the proxying, retries and session rotation — we wrote none of it.
  • Pay-per-run means the whole watchlist costs cents per refresh, so Polaris can re-check the city as often as the city changes.
  • A second actor crawls public calendar pages as prose, indexed alongside the structured events for questions the schema can't answer.

Honest note: the actor we first picked turned out to be keyword-only and behind a paid rental. We swapped to the official profile scraper — that's why the eyes take profile URLs, not search terms.

08
Demo · five minutes

What you'll see.

  1. Tonight, on the phone. Austin's dance scene for tonight — every card carrying a verdict, not just a listing.
  2. Tap a cancelled badge. It opens the actual Instagram post. The calendar said it was on; the venue said closed.
  3. Ask it something vague. "beginner friendly country night under $10" — semantic retrieval finds the honky-tonk that never says those words.
  4. Re-run the agent live. Scrape, reconcile, and watch a badge change on screen while you're looking at it.
  5. Kibana. The perception log and the verdict split — the agent's memory, inspectable.
